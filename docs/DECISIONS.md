# DEEP TIME — Decision Log

Every autonomous decision is recorded here with a short rationale. Newest at the bottom.

## Phase 1

- **D1 — Renderer: `three/webgpu` WebGPURenderer + TSL everywhere.** One shader language that compiles to WGSL (WebGPU) and GLSL (WebGL2 backend), so the fallback is automatic and identical. Verified in this container: headless Chromium exposes a SwiftShader WebGPU adapter on `localhost` with `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`, and WebGL2 via ANGLE/SwiftShader, so both backends are testable.
- **D2 — three r187, `RenderPipeline`** (PostProcessing was renamed in r183). Two `PassNode`s with swappable scene/camera give live cross-stage transitions (match cuts) without rebuilding the graph.
- **D3 — Compute is optional, never required.** GPU particle motion is analytic in the vertex stage (orbits, curl-noise drift, density-wave spirals) or uses render-target ping-pong (reaction–diffusion), which run identically on both backends. Compute shaders are only used where an equivalent non-compute path exists.
- **D4 — Sim time unit**: years relative to 2026 CE (float64). "Present" is 2026 CE, the Singularity spans 2026–2076.
- **D5 — Odyssey interpolation** is a monotone cubic in signed-log time `z = sign(T)·log10(1+|T|)`: C1 speed, no overshoot, log-paced for free, exactly invertible for the timeline.
- **D6 — Timeline bar is piecewise-logarithmic**: chapters occupy widths proportional to their Odyssey budget and time inside each chapter is log-warped. A literal log axis from 4 Gya to 10^100 would devote 90 % of the bar to the empty far future.
- **D7 — Agents run in local time.** At geological speeds real generation times are invisible, so agents tick at a fixed 30 Hz local clock and the fate field keeps their aggregates on the canonical track. This is what makes "slow down and they come alive" work at every scale.
- **D8 — Regime B is precomputed ahead of playback.** The galaxy worker starts computing the run's history as soon as the seed is known (at Begin) and streams events/checkpoints; the Director can therefore "edit" the emergent war (cut to the decisive battle) and stretch the Contest up to 60 s.
- **D9 — The Heir.** Earth's AI is one faction among 4–7 in the Contest and can lose. Rivals: Lattice, Choir, Hexarchy, Mycelium, Veil, Furnace, Archive (3–6 per seed).
- **D10 — The Tone is A3 = 220 Hz** (audible on laptop speakers, unlike 110 Hz) at −42 dBFS; the Return chord is an open A add9 voicing.
- **D11 — Typeface: Lexend Exa** (SIL OFL 1.1, bundled from @fontsource), weights 100/200/300.
- **D12 — No hard letterbox.** Frames are composed for 16:9 with a 2.2:1 action-safe band so every frame can be a wallpaper.
- **D13 — Deploy**: the GitHub tooling in this environment cannot change repository Pages settings (no `gh api` for non-search calls), so the workflow uses `actions/configure-pages` with `enablement: true` and the README documents the one-time "Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions" step.

## Phase 2

- **D14 — Headless captures use the WebGL2 backend.** In this container, WebGPU canvases are never composited into headless screenshots (even a raw WebGPU triangle captures blank) and three's `pushErrorScope/popErrorScope` trips a SwiftShader/Dawn "Instance dropped" quirk. Because all shaders are TSL, the WebGL2 backend renders the same images, so Playwright screenshots and e2e runs pass `?backend=webgl`. Real browsers still get WebGPU first, with automatic fallback to WebGL2 on adapter failure or device loss.
- **D15 — TSL shader graphs are authored through an `any` façade** (`src/render/tsl/T.ts`); @types/three's TSL generics reject valid graphs (e.g. colour uniforms in vec3 math). All non-shader code stays strict.

## Phases 3–4

- **D16 — Lean gather DOF instead of three's DOF node.** The built-in node produced white frames on the WebGL2 backend; a 12-tap golden-angle gather using the A-pass depth (`perspectiveDepthToViewZ`) is cheap and identical on both backends.
- **D17 — Shared 64³ tileable value-noise texture** (`vnoise`, `vfbm`) replaces ALU noise in raymarchers and big sprites: an order of magnitude faster in SwiftShader, and kind to low-end GPUs.
- **D18 — Analytic fluorescence shells for the endosymbiosis cell** (ray–ellipsoid hits) plus a coarse 22-step volume, instead of a fine raymarch that lost the device.
- **D19 — Cambrian swimmers are position-based-dynamics segment chains** with resistive-force drag (anisotropic, à la slender-body theory): stable at any time step and gaits emerge from the genome's phase/amplitude genes.
- **D20 — Vent geometry is meshed with surface nets in a worker** (3 s of main-thread SDF meshing moved off-thread; the Prologue frame stays bit-identical because the mesh is deterministic).
- **D21 — Night lights are a density field broken into points by two speckle octaves**, with continuous city cores, so the same layer serves fires (hominins), villages (ancient), cities (modern) and their extinction (Singularity).
- **D22 — Per-era local-rule sims**: microbes, colonies/creatures, ecosystem stress, hominin bands, polities, cities, lattice sparks — each a pure-TS AgentSim behind one worker, each with a FateField that pins its aggregates to the canonical track.
- **D23 — The lattice is crystal growth.** Each datacenter nucleates a grain with its own orientation; the grain is a regular triangular lattice at three nested scales in azimuthal-equidistant coordinates, anti-aliased with `fwidth`; grain boundaries glow where crystals meet. It is evaluated only once it exists (uniform `If`), so other eras pay nothing.
- **D24 — The last light is Tristan da Cunha** (−37.07, −12.31), the most remote inhabited island; the lattice leaves a closing hole around it until 2048.
- **D25 — The solar system is not to scale** (Sun r = 0.3, swarm 0.6–1.9): legibility first. The swarm is 24k instanced collectors whose lit faces show across the far side and whose backs glow with waste heat; it ends framed as an ellipse for the match cut to the galactic disk.

## Phase 5

- **D26 — Local Group graph**: 24 000 nodes (MW 17k, LMC 1.1k, SMC 0.5k, M31 4.3k, M33 1.1k), k=7 nearest neighbours per galaxy, built deterministically in ~0.4 s. Galaxy centres move (the Clouds merge at +2.4 Gyr, Andromeda passes at +4.5 Gyr and merges by +6.5 Gyr); cross-galaxy wars use probe launches and invasion fleets, so adjacency stays per-galaxy.
- **D27 — Light-delayed belief**: each faction's view of a rival is the rival's snapshot from one light-travel-time ago (minimum sample-pair distance / c). RKVs and beams are aimed at those stale cores and hit whoever is there on arrival — miscalculation and strikes on vanished targets are emergent, not scripted.
- **D28 — Fronts move by net pressure**, evaluated per edge from each side (only the stronger side advances), with a consolidation window after capture; this removed ping-pong churn and made wars resolve. Mobilisation in a war scales as (P_A/P_B)^0.22, rising to ^0.72 in the endgame so stalemates break; a stall detector forces exhaustion armistices.
- **D29 — The Heir is everywhere but thin** (capMult 0.32): it colonises the Local Group in ~20 Myr, but awakenings seize 300–1200 of its systems and intruders 600–2000, so it wins about a third of runs.
- **D30 — Rare doomsday branches**: total war (two reckless great powers saturating each other, poisoning the ground for 3 Gyr) produces annihilation outcomes; false-vacuum decay is a hazard per year for desperate high-risk factions and black-hole engineers (~5–7 % of runs).
- **D31 — Balance**: 50 seeds (`npm run test:balance`, also in CI) must show ≥3 outcome types, ≥4 distinct winners, and no goal, megastructure or faction winning more than 42 % of runs. The default `npm test` runs an 8-seed subset to stay fast.
- **D32 — The chronicle is retroactive where it must be**: "went silent … It did not answer again" is only written if the faction indeed never re-emerged (the whole history exists before it is shown). Repeated lines per pair are muted for hundreds of Myr.
- **D33 — The Contest is edited, not just played**: `buildContest` picks up to nine beats by weight (awakenings, betrayals, extinctions, the collision, the bubble), gives each screen time ∝ weight, and builds both the Odyssey time keys and the shots (regional, galactic, grazing, collision framings) from the run's own history.
- **D34 — Galaxy rendering in kpc**: ~800k stars (high tier) with flux-conserving minimum pixel size, emission nebulae, absorbing dust sprites, per-node territory sprites sized to local node spacing whose patterns are evaluated in world space (each faction's visual language in its own shader branch), interference moiré on contested fronts, streaks for RKVs/probes/fleets, beams, novae, and a vacuum bubble that erases everything inside it.

## Phases 6–7

- **D35 — Captures run against `vite preview` (the built `dist/`), never the dev server**: Vite's hot reload restarted pages mid-capture while code was being edited, which produced flaky white frames and timeouts.
- **D36 — The hands-free pacing run is the end-to-end test** (`scripts/odyssey-run.mjs`): it found the microbial field divergence (dead air) that no deep-link capture could, because it needs the history of a continuous run. Capture-mode frames may step up to 1 s so a full run fits in about an hour of software rendering.
- **D37 — Bounded integrators everywhere a fate projection can rescale a field**: Gray–Scott values are clamped to [0,1] each step, projections rescale by at most 3× and top up the remainder uniformly so milestones still land exactly.
- **D38 — Post grade**: AgX output is expanded ×1.1 (clamped) so hero emitters reach display white; saturation 1.28 to restore the palettes AgX desaturates; non-finite pixels are cleaned before bloom.
- **D39 — Contest legibility over literalism**: an empire's interior is a whisper (presence-weighted: small powers are drawn brighter), contested fronts are two-colour interference fringes, and the chronicle drops any line that has fallen behind the picture.
- **D40 — The Return ring closes around the first cell**: the timeline curls into a ring centred on the protocell's projected position, so the end visibly meets the beginning.
- **D41 — Consistent sun side per chapter** (right for chapters 1–3, left for 4–7) so cuts between orbital shots don't jump; night globes use varied framings (limb arcs, regional close-ups, a small disc with negative space).
- **D42 — Every Contest beat has a protagonist**: the beat's event names one power (the dying one for an extinction); while the beat is on screen its territory is lifted and every other power recedes, its event is drawn in its colour (ignition ring, collapsing ring, slow pulse), and its own dated chronicle line is shown regardless of what else the stream holds.
- **D43 — Fronts are banded along the border at a screen-fixed period** (~1.4 % of frame height, from the rig's look distance), oriented low→high faction index so both sides agree; never over a galactic core.
- **D44 — Disk stars follow an exponential surface density** (Gamma(2) radii, a quarter on the old steeper profile) so the strategic graph and the visible disk have the same extent and the Sun sits at half radius.
- **D45 — The opening frame is centred**: the ember sits just below frame centre and the membrane is centred, so the Return's ring closes concentrically around the first cell.
- **D46 — Transitions sweep across the disc**: a sky mid-transition (the GOE) clears toward one side of the frame instead of changing uniformly, so the event reads in a single still.
- **D47 — Drama is a grammar, not a garnish** (Phase 9): every great turn uses the same four strokes — build (a riser peaking exactly on the beat, a growing camera tremor), inhale (the music drops out; the Tone stays), hit (sub drop, boom, crack, camera kick, the stage's own flash), let go (a dated line; the music returns lower over 2.5 s). Beats live in data (`src/chapters/beats.ts`), anchored to a moment inside a shot or to a sim time; the Contest's beats are derived from its own history (`CONTEST_STROKES`).
- **D48 — Regime A speaks**: two to four dated lines per chapter at the turns only, in the chronicle's typography; facts are real; a new line retires the previous one. The Coda stays wordless.
- **D49 — The drama bus**: risers, hits, heartbeats and the rupture bypass the silence gate (a hit must land out of silence) and have their own room; the music bus keeps the gate.
- **D50 — The score follows the story**: the palettes' state hints (dread, impact winter, fire, plague, industry, the human lights, the AI) were never fed and are now driven from world state and the beat engine; a heartbeat runs from 2034 to the last human light, quickening from 54 to 118 bpm; the Contest gains a pulse that quickens with tension.
- **D51 — Black beats and the rupture**: held blacks with silence before "We built a mind." and "It was not alone."; the takeover tears the picture (displaced slices, split colour) and stutters the sound before the colder world begins.
- **D52 — Text hygiene is enforced in code** (review pass D): one dated line at a time; a line waits until the previous one has been on screen for 1.5 s + 0.3 s per word, and is dropped if it would wait more than 2.6 s; the page clears a second before every chapter turn and stays clear through its card (longer before the black beats); the Expansion is told by its own few lines (the raw event stream starts with the Contest); cards get a soft dark backing.
- **D53 — The Contest has a protagonist and an ending**: the mind we built is "It" to the end, every rival is named by where it came from ("the mind in Andromeda", "the mind from the dark", "the new light"); the protagonist's death is always its own beat and the Contest's climax (built, inhaled, hit at 1.0), after which the war of strangers gets one more beat at most; the closing shot says who was left ("It was alone again." / "Only the mind from the dark was left."). Hits are rationed: full strokes only for first contact, the protagonist's fate and the finale.
- **D54 — The middle no longer sags**: six seconds move from Ancient Civilizations to the Modern World; the cradles ignite one by one (each city flares as it is founded); the plague is built up to and its wake goes nearly dark; the departure in 1969 gets ~3.5 s with "Someone left." on screen while the mote still climbs.
- **D55 — The score builds, not just punctuates**: the bed swells ~4–5 dB into Chicxulub, Trinity, the protagonist's death and the finale, and sits ~3.5 dB lower for six seconds after.
